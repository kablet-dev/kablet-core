# Kablet Blueprint — Chapter 01: Business

**Status:** Locked product and business direction (2026-10-10); updated dual-path ecommerce launch decision supersedes earlier bridge-only launch plan  
**Scope:** Vision, problem, market, positioning, offering, monetization, distribution, onboarding, economics, and validation.  
**Next chapters:** 02 Intelligence; 03 Execution; 04 Experience; 05 Learning; 06 Infrastructure.

> **Interpretation:** “Locked” records an agreed strategic decision, not a claim that functionality is already built, commercially proven, or legally cleared. Targets, hypotheses, and deferred decisions are explicitly labeled.

## Executive definition

**Brand:** Kablet — **The AI Frontend for Business**.

**Product:** A **Conversion Intelligence System** that owns and operates the complete customer-facing frontend, dynamically adapting experiences to visitor intent to drive measurable business outcomes. Kablet is not a chatbot, widget, theme, overlay, or ordinary website builder.

**Mission:** Turn visitor intent into measurable commercial outcomes through an AI-native frontend.

**Governing principle:** **The Merchant Owns the Truth. Kablet Owns the Experience.** Merchants own factual business information, goals, policies, permissions, and approvals; Kablet owns experience composition, sequencing, and permitted adaptation. Platform safety boundaries outrank merchant-approved policies, which outrank runtime decisions.

**First commercial vertical:** Ecommerce, with **two mandatory self-service launch paths**: connect a supported existing commerce backend (headless), or create a fully operational minimal Kablet-native store. **Long-term destination:** Industry-neutral AI-native business platform spanning ecommerce, digital products, bookings, B2B, services, and other verticals.

**Commercial proof:** Verified completed purchases and credible incremental **revenue per visitor (RPV)**, with conversion-rate improvement as a key diagnostic and early proof milestone.

## 1.1 Vision & Identity — LOCKED

### Why Kablet exists

Static websites present largely predetermined customer journeys even when visitors have very different needs, levels of intent, and buying readiness. Kablet's long-term vision is to replace static digital frontends with intelligent experiences that respond to each visitor while remaining grounded in the business's facts and constraints.

### Locked decisions

1. Position Kablet as **“The AI Frontend for Business.”**
2. Kablet **replaces and owns the complete customer-facing frontend**, rather than acting as a chatbot, widget, script, overlay, theme, or superficial personalization layer.
3. The mission is to turn visitor intent into measurable commercial outcomes through an AI-native frontend.
4. Start commercially with ecommerce while designing the underlying platform to serve multiple business verticals.
5. **The Merchant Owns the Truth. Kablet Owns the Experience.** Merchant authority covers catalog/services, prices, availability, inventory, brand, fulfillment and return policies, objectives, permissions, and approval boundaries. Kablet governs layouts, components, sequencing, and adaptation within those boundaries.
6. Authority ordering: **platform safety > merchant-approved policies > runtime decisions**. Kablet cannot invent unauthorized discounts, guarantees, terms, or business facts.

### Non-goals

Do not reposition Kablet as a standalone chat assistant, drag-and-drop page builder, template library, or thin AI layer over a conventional site.

## 1.2 The Problem — LOCKED

### Customer problem hypothesis

Conventional ecommerce sites can create friction when a predefined experience does not fit the visitor's question, readiness, or purchase context. Search, filters, recommendations, and chat already solve parts of this problem; Kablet's proposed advantage is **coordinated adaptation of the complete experience**, not the claim that every existing storefront is poor.

Hypothesized issues to validate:

- Visitor intent and page presentation may not align.
- Visitors can encounter avoidable discovery, comparison, and checkout friction.
- Assistance and merchandising decisions are often fragmented across tools.
- Storefronts may have limited ability to adapt coherently in real time.
- Merchants may struggle to link experience changes to verified commercial outcomes.

### Locked decisions

1. **Revenue-first positioning:** “Kablet helps ecommerce businesses generate more revenue from their existing visitors by adapting the shopping experience to each customer's intent.”
2. **Autonomy is the mechanism; merchant revenue is the reason to buy.**
3. Measure verified completed purchases and **RPV = attributed revenue / eligible visitors**; also track conversion rate (CVR), average order value (AOV), and contribution profit where available.
4. Incremental improvement must be demonstrated through trustworthy measurement, not assumed from a compelling demonstration.

**Evidence status:** Revenue uplift is a product hypothesis, not a proven result.

## 1.3 Target Market & Ideal Customer — LOCKED

### Market sequence

- **First vertical:** Ecommerce merchants.
- **First pilot selection:** **Opportunity-led**, not restricted to one store size or category.
- **Later repeatable ICP:** Narrow based on actual adoption, measurable value, and retention.
- **Long-term:** Digital products, bookings, services, and other business models supported by the universal platform.

### Pilot merchant criteria

Prioritize a cooperative merchant with meaningful traffic, real purchase data, observable purchase-decision friction, the ability to run a safe frontend experiment, and willingness to share outcomes. Existing platform integrations may lower initial deployment risk.

### Proof-oriented early pilot recruitment targets — locked planning guidance

For the first 1–3 **commercial proof pilots**, preferentially recruit merchants with approximately **5,000+ monthly visitors**, **$50,000+ monthly revenue**, active paid acquisition, measurable checkout/purchase events, and a clearly expressed conversion problem. These are **screening heuristics, not statistically sufficient sample sizes, hard platform eligibility rules, or validated ICP boundaries**. Actual experiment feasibility depends on baseline purchase counts, traffic allocation, desired detectable lift, variance, attribution quality, and test duration; calculate statistical power before promising a verification timeline. A merchant below either heuristic may be an excellent pilot if it offers a stronger test opportunity. Self-service availability should not exclude smaller merchants merely for being smaller. Prioritize early credible evidence without redefining Kablet as a high-revenue-only product.

**Shopify** is a preferred initial integration candidate, not a permanent dependency or exclusive requirement. Other commerce providers may be added via adapters.

### Locked decisions

1. Choose **Option B: opportunity-led pilot selection**.
2. Do not lock a particular ecommerce product category or merchant revenue band without evidence.
3. Refine a repeatable ICP only after early experiments reveal which merchants benefit and can adopt successfully.
4. Account for adoption risk: replacing an entire frontend is a material merchant decision.

**Not locked:** Earlier example ranges of $30k–$300k monthly merchant revenue and 10k–100k monthly visitors were illustrative only.

## 1.4 Value Proposition & Differentiation — LOCKED

### Core promise

**Kablet is a Conversion Intelligence System that owns and operates the complete customer-facing frontend.** It seeks to increase revenue from existing traffic by matching experience decisions to visitor intent and improving those decisions with commercial evidence.

### Three pillars

1. **Commercial outcomes:** Revenue per visitor and verified purchases matter more than AI novelty.
2. **Intelligent experience autonomy:** Kablet controls and coordinates the entire permitted customer-facing experience.
3. **Evidence-based improvement:** Experience policies evolve through observed outcomes and credible experiments.

### Merchant trust positioning

**The Merchant Owns the Truth. Kablet Owns the Experience.** This is a primary homepage and merchant-facing positioning line. Merchants control authoritative products, prices, inventory, commercial permissions and approvals; Kablet adapts the customer-facing experience within those boundaries. Illustrative demo: when discretionary discounts are prohibited, a visitor asking for 20% off cannot cause Kablet to create an unauthorized discount; it may present only valid existing promotions. Kablet must provide controlled pause, rollback, and recovery for unsafe or defective policies, with recovery guarantees stated only when supported by operational evidence.

### Competitive positioning

Compared with storefront builders, chatbots, personalization tools, and A/B testing products, Kablet aims to integrate intent understanding, adaptive composition, transaction execution, verified outcomes, and policy improvement within one frontend runtime. Existing products can overlap with individual or multiple capabilities; no exclusive market claim is assumed.

### Locked decisions

1. Primary competitive identity: **Option B — Conversion Intelligence System**.
2. Kablet is not merely an AI copy generator, analytics dashboard, unrestricted autonomous agent, or chat widget.
3. Potential defensibility comes from accumulated decision-to-outcome evidence, improved policies, reliable merchant integrations, and embedded workflows.
4. **Defensibility remains a hypothesis to earn**, not an established moat.

## 1.5 Product Offering — LOCKED

### One integrated product

Kablet ultimately combines:

1. **Adaptive frontend runtime:** Entire customer-facing site and contextual experience composition.
2. **Business truth and configuration:** Products, services, prices, policies, branding, goals, and constraints.
3. **Business Intelligence Manager:** Merchant-side governance of objectives, strategies, permissions, and approvals.
4. **Business integrations and actions:** Commerce, checkout, bookings, and other supported execution capabilities.
5. **Performance and revenue reporting:** Verified outcomes and commercial evidence.
6. **Managed intelligence and optimization:** Intent, policy selection, experiments, and evidence-based improvement.

These describe intended product components; not all are asserted to be implemented.

### Dual-path ecommerce launch — both required on Day 1

**Path 1 — Connect existing commerce (headless):** The merchant connects Shopify or another explicitly supported provider. Kablet owns the entire customer-facing adaptive frontend, while the connected backend remains the authoritative system for supported catalog, inventory, checkout/payment, orders, and fulfillment operations. Use provider-neutral commerce interfaces and adapters; no permanent Shopify dependency.

**Path 2 — Build directly on Kablet (native):** A merchant with no existing store creates and operates a real ecommerce business entirely on Kablet. This is a **Day 1 public-release requirement**, not a later phase. Native commerce is deliberately minimal but operational, with:

- Product creation/editing, pricing, images, and essential variants.
- Basic inventory and availability tracking.
- Functional cart, checkout, and supported payment-processor integration.
- Order recording, payment status, and basic order administration.
- Basic shipping destinations/rates and fulfillment information for supported physical-goods markets.
- Applicable tax configuration/calculation for supported launch jurisdictions.
- Merchant identity, policies, contact information, and essential transactional notifications.
- Supported cancellation/refund handling and essential operational safeguards.
- Store preview, custom domain/HTTPS, essential SEO, analytics, and verified conversion measurement.

**Not required for Day 1:** Shopify feature parity, enterprise warehouse management, hundreds of integrations, advanced promotion engines, or sophisticated multi-market operations. Supported markets, payment processors, tax/shipping coverage, and operational limits must be declared honestly and validated before launch.

**Shared intelligence, different commerce implementations:** Both paths use the **same Kablet adaptive frontend, intent intelligence, governance, action contracts, verified outcomes, and learning architecture**. A provider-neutral commerce interface separates the visitor experience from native or external operational backends. Kablet must not invent prices, inventory, delivery promises, or other business facts.

**Cross-vertical clarification:** Ecommerce is only the **first vertical to master**, not the platform's definition. Universal onboarding and core architecture must accommodate digital products, bookings, B2B, services, business websites, and future business models. Each new vertical launches only when its actual execution capabilities are production-ready; no Shopify requirement for unrelated verticals.

### V1 SEO and traffic-routing strategy — locked

**Headless / connected-store path:** For the initial V1 deployment, the merchant’s existing Shopify (or supported provider) storefront remains the **organic-search-facing canonical site**. Kablet serves **selected paid/social campaign traffic** through a compatible landing URL, subdomain, or other verified routing arrangement, without immediately replacing the merchant’s organic storefront. This is a controlled traffic-segmentation strategy, **not** a claim that Kablet is a small widget or a partial frontend: Kablet owns the **complete customer-facing experience for traffic assigned to Kablet**, while the incumbent storefront continues serving its retained traffic. Keep SEO authority and canonical ownership unambiguous; avoid duplicate indexable catalog pages, conflicting canonicals, and accidental organic-traffic displacement. Verify domain/cookie/checkout handoff, attribution, analytics, consent, and provider compatibility before publishing. Broader domain or organic-traffic migration is a later, explicitly approved deployment step after validation.

**Kablet-native ecommerce path:** Kablet owns the entire public storefront **and its SEO from Day 1**. The native site must deliver crawlable, meaningful HTML (via server-side rendering **or** appropriate static prerendering), stable product/category URLs, unique metadata, canonical URLs, sitemap, robots directives, structured data where relevant, and HTTPS/custom-domain readiness. The exact rendering implementation (SSR versus prerendering or hybrid) is an **Infrastructure Chapter decision**; the business requirement is a production-ready indexable storefront, not client-side-only pages that rely on search crawlers to execute JavaScript.

**Shared rule:** Both paths must support trustworthy conversion measurement and safe launch checks. SEO traffic and paid/social traffic must be distinguishable in reporting. The initial headless SEO arrangement is a **V1 rollout constraint**, not a permanent limit on Kablet’s ability to replace an entire frontend.

### First proof boundary

One real ecommerce storefront with trustworthy catalog information, differentiated discovery/comparison/ready-to-buy experiences, functional cart/checkout, verified purchase events, and ideally a controlled comparison against its existing frontend.

### Locked decisions

1. Ultimate destination: **Option C — Universal AI Business Platform**, developed incrementally.
2. **Both ecommerce paths are mandatory for the first public release:** existing-backend headless integration and minimal but operational Kablet-native ecommerce.
3. Native commerce must support real end-to-end orders and payments without requiring the merchant to sign up for Shopify; feature parity with Shopify is not required.
4. Both paths share the same frontend and intelligence through provider-neutral commerce contracts.
5. Business truth and merchant authority remain separate from Kablet's experience autonomy.
6. Ecommerce is the first vertical, while Kablet's long-term architecture and guided onboarding remain industry-neutral.
7. **V1 SEO split:** Connected/headless merchants retain their incumbent SEO storefront while Kablet initially serves selected paid/social traffic; native Kablet stores own indexable SEO-ready storefronts from Day 1. Rendering method remains an infrastructure choice.

## 1.6 Business Model & Pricing — LOCKED DIRECTION; PRICES TO VALIDATE

### Initial revenue model

**Option B — Subscription + Usage.** Merchants pay a recurring platform subscription with an included allowance of eligible visitor sessions and transparent usage overages. Merchant bills should not be denominated in AI tokens or raw model calls.

- **Proposed entry subscription:** **$299/month minimum target**, subject to market validation; not a finalized published price.
- **Usage:** Define eligible sessions, bot exclusions, metering, included volume, overages, and billing safeguards before release.
- **Later GMV layer:** A strategic target of **0.5%–1% of qualifying GMV** processed through Kablet's future native commerce/checkout, if commercially justified and compliant. Owning a checkout UI does not by itself entitle Kablet to transaction fees.
- **Not at launch:** Performance-based uplift billing; credible incrementality attribution would be required to support it.

### Cost discipline

Use deterministic, low-cost routine decisions, selective AI for high-value interpretation, caching where appropriate, and asynchronous evidence-based learning. Internal AI costs must be observable and budgeted even though merchants are not billed per token.

### Locked decisions

1. Launch with subscription plus eligible-session usage.
2. Treat $299/month as a **proposed minimum target**, not proven willingness to pay.
3. Reserve native-commerce GMV fees as a future optional revenue layer, not a launch promise.
4. Avoid disputed uplift-based billing at launch.

**Open:** Exact tiers, included sessions, overage rates, trial terms, refunds, taxation, and native GMV-fee eligibility.

## 1.8 Merchant Onboarding & Success — LOCKED

### Guiding architecture

**Self-service from the first public release.** One universal onboarding system branches by business type and supported capabilities rather than assuming every merchant is a Shopify store.

**Illustrative business-type selection:** Physical ecommerce, digital products, bookings/services, business website/lead generation. The interface may expose only paths that are actually production-ready; unsupported paths must not be falsely offered as working products.

### Guided paths

1. **Choose business type and objective.** Ask only relevant questions.
2. **Choose creation source.** Connect an existing supported provider or build directly on Kablet where native capabilities exist.
3. **Provide or sync business truth.** Catalog/services, inventory/availability, pricing, policies, branding, business details, and permissions.
4. **Set objectives and authority.** Merchant-approved constraints, supported actions, and required approvals.
5. **Generate and preview.** Kablet composes a functional frontend; merchant verifies facts and behavior rather than manually designing pages.
6. **Verify commerce or service actions.** Test checkout/booking, event attribution, and supported integration behavior.
7. **Launch readiness.** Domain configuration if applicable, HTTPS, essential SEO, analytics, privacy, monitoring, and rollback/transition plan.
8. **Publish with merchant approval.** Use a safe deployment/traffic rollout where feasible.
9. **Ongoing success.** Report activation, verified purchases, revenue outcomes, policy governance, and supported optimization.

### Initial release scope

**Updated and locked: Dual-path ecommerce from Day 1.** The first public release must offer (1) self-service connection to at least one supported external commerce backend, and (2) self-service creation of a complete **minimal operational Kablet-native store**. Both must pass real end-to-end purchase verification. Native commerce is not deferred. The guided onboarding framework remains universal, while other verticals activate only when supported and production-ready.

### Domain and SEO timing

- Provide a Kablet preview address during setup.
- Before public launch, complete required domain/HTTPS, analytics, privacy, and transactional checks. For **native Kablet storefronts**, also complete crawlability, titles/meta, sitemap/robots, canonicals and relevant structured data. For **V1 headless paid/social deployments**, preserve incumbent SEO ownership, use suitable indexing/canonical controls for Kablet traffic pages, and verify routing and attribution.
- For migrations, address URL preservation, redirects, indexing risks, and rollback **before** routing production traffic.
- Advanced SEO/content growth and optional integrations continue after launch.
- Do not promise a fixed “five-minute launch” without real validation.

### Activation and success definitions

- **Technical activation:** Accurate business data and reliable end-to-end live experience.
- **Commercial activation:** Real eligible visitors and verified transactions/outcomes.
- **Proven merchant value:** Credible incremental commercial benefit, not merely a functional site.

### Locked decisions

1. Self-service is a Day 1 release requirement for **supported** capabilities.
2. Universal, business-type-guided architecture; no single-vertical onboarding assumption.
3. **Two selectable ecommerce paths from Day 1:** supported existing-provider integration or Kablet-native store creation.
4. Native ecommerce needs the minimal reliable commerce operations listed in Section 1.5, not Shopify-scale feature parity.
5. Preview before production; domain, SEO, measurement, and transaction checks are launch-readiness concerns.
6. Merchant retains business-policy authority and final launch approval.
7. Never advertise an unsupported path as operational.

## 1.9 Business Metrics & Unit Economics — LOCKED

### Measurement philosophy

**Option B — Contribution Profit + Retention** is Kablet's primary internal business-health philosophy. The early-stage operating north star is **Time to First Verified CVR Lift**. The product's primary commercial outcome remains **verified incremental RPV**; a CVR lift that harms revenue per visitor, order quality, or merchant profitability is not sufficient proof of value.

### Five-metric early-stage scorecard

| Metric | Definition / interpretation |
|---|---|
| Time to First Verified CVR Lift | Elapsed time from merchant signup to the first **credible, verified** positive conversion result; distinguish observed lift from adequately supported evidence. |
| Verified CVR Improvement | Relative or absolute conversion change versus an appropriate comparator, with uncertainty and test design recorded. |
| Month-2 Merchant Retention | Percentage of eligible merchant cohort still active/paying in the second month, with a consistent cohort definition. |
| Contribution Margin per Merchant | Merchant revenue less attributable AI, infrastructure, integration, support, and other direct service costs, divided by merchant revenue; also track dollar contribution. |
| Monthly Recurring Revenue | Committed recurring subscription and usage minimums; variable usage overages separately reported rather than automatically treated as committed MRR. |

### Supporting measurements

- Verified incremental **RPV** and purchase/order integrity as commercial guardrails.
- Activation rate, time to launch, time to first verified purchase, and experiment duration.
- Eligible visitor sessions; AI and infrastructure cost per eligible session; AI cost per assisted session and verified purchase.
- Merchant churn, gross retention, net revenue retention (NRR), and expansion.
- Fully loaded acquisition cost (CAC), contribution-based CAC payback, and retention-adjusted lifetime value when enough data exists.

**Interpretation caveat:** Time to verified lift depends on merchant traffic and statistical power; do not penalize low-traffic stores simply for taking longer to accumulate evidence. Never count a short-lived observed lift as verified causal improvement.

### Economic assumptions, not facts

- Early ecommerce merchant **CAC planning scenario: $300–$500**. This is not a measured benchmark.
- **$299/month** remains a proposed entry subscription, not confirmed realized pricing.
- Illustrative cost-per-session models are scenario analyses, not actual Kablet telemetry.

### Locked decisions

1. Contribution profit and retention lead internal business-health evaluation.
2. Time to first **verified** CVR lift is the early operating north star.
3. Incremental RPV remains the primary commercial outcome and guardrail.
4. Use a focused five-metric headline scorecard, with supporting cost and attribution diagnostics.
5. Set numerical operating targets after credible data rather than inventing thresholds.

## 1.10 Validation & Growth Strategy — LOCKED

### Chosen approach

**Option A — Evidence-Gated Growth.** Kablet expands only when the preceding product, commercial, and economic claims are sufficiently supported. Milestones are evidence-based rather than tied to arbitrary calendar dates.

### Five validation stages

1. **Working product:** One real ecommerce storefront operates with accurate business data, adaptive discovery/comparison/ready-to-buy experiences, reliable cart/checkout, verified purchases, and safety/rollback safeguards.
2. **First commercial evidence:** Measure a real merchant deployment with an appropriate comparator and assess CVR, RPV, order quality, and reliability.
3. **Repeatability:** Seek credible evidence across multiple merchants or independent tests; do not generalize from one favorable experiment.
4. **Distribution and retention:** Prove supported self-service activation, repeatable merchant acquisition, ongoing retention, and viable contribution economics.
5. **Platform expansion:** Expand the Day 1 minimal native-commerce capabilities and introduce additional verticals when readiness and customer value justify them.

### First commercial experiment

**Question:** For comparable visitors, does Kablet's adaptive frontend produce a meaningfully better shopping outcome than the merchant's existing storefront?

Where feasible, use consistent randomized assignment between existing-storefront control and Kablet treatment. Establish eligible visitors, attribution windows, primary RPV outcome, CVR diagnostics, sample-size considerations, guardrails, and stopping/analysis rules before looking at results. Verify completed purchases rather than trusting frontend-only success events. A negative or inconclusive result is learning, not license to make a positive claim.

### Gates before aggressive scaling

- Product and transaction reliability.
- Credible commercial evidence, or an explicit learning plan for unresolved evidence.
- Merchant retention and perceived value.
- Sustainable merchant-level contribution economics.
- Repeatable distribution and self-service activation without custom engineering per merchant.

### Growth priorities

1. **More ecommerce merchants** on the validated frontend/integration path.
2. **Richer native commerce capabilities** beyond the Day 1 operational minimum, when economically and operationally justified.
3. **More business verticals** reusing shared intelligence and experience primitives.

These are priorities, not absolute sequencing prohibitions. An adjacent vertical may be tested earlier if low-cost and non-distracting.

### Locked decisions

1. **Option A — Evidence-Gated Growth**, not simultaneous aggressive platform expansion.
2. First proof must involve a real ecommerce store and verified transaction outcomes.
3. Compare against an appropriate existing-storefront control where feasible.
4. Validate reliability, merchant value, retention, economics, and repeatable acquisition before aggressive scaling.
5. Expand **native commerce depth beyond its Day 1 minimum** and add new verticals in response to validated demand and production readiness.

## Consolidated strategic commitments

1. **Identity:** Full AI-native frontend replacement and Conversion Intelligence System; never merely a widget.
2. **Commercial objective:** Merchant revenue improvement, verified through RPV and purchases; CVR lift is an important early proof signal.
3. **Merchant authority:** Business facts, policy, permissions, and approvals belong to the merchant; Kablet owns adaptive experience execution within limits.
4. **Market entry:** Ecommerce first, opportunity-led pilots, provider-agnostic integration contracts.
5. **Product launch:** Ecommerce first with **both headless integration and minimal functional Kablet-native commerce from Day 1**; universal multi-vertical architecture from the start.
6. **Monetization:** Subscription + eligible-session usage; proposed $299 entry target; future conditional native GMV layer.
7. **Distribution:** Pull-led content, demos, ecosystems, case studies, and referrals after credible proof.
8. **Onboarding:** Universal guided **self-service Day 1** for supported paths, with preview, domain/SEO readiness, and safe merchant-approved publishing.
9. **Economics:** Contribution profit + retention, with early north star time to first verified CVR lift and incremental RPV guardrail.
10. **Growth:** Evidence-gated milestones rather than premature breadth or arbitrary deadlines.

## Explicitly unproven, deferred, or open

- **Unproven:** Incremental revenue uplift, repeatability across stores, actual CAC, willingness to pay, conversion-intelligence defensibility, and native-commerce demand.
- **Pricing to validate:** Final plans, included sessions, overage schedule, trials, discounts, and merchant billing rules.
- **Technical/provider choices to validate:** First supported commerce provider(s), integration permissions, deployment architecture, storefront compatibility, checkout limitations, attribution mechanics, and migration safeguards.
- **Native ecommerce required at launch:** The minimum viable native catalog, inventory, cart, payment/checkout, orders, shipping/tax for supported markets, and merchant operations must be implemented, tested, and verified before the first public release. **Advanced** native-commerce features remain deferred.
- **Other verticals deferred until supported:** Digital goods, booking/services, business websites, and their corresponding execution and compliance requirements.
- **Experiment standards to specify:** Eligibility, randomization, statistical design, RPV and CVR definitions, significance/uncertainty criteria, fraud/bot filtering, attribution windows, and operational guardrails.
- **Operational targets to establish:** Cost ceilings, uptime, activation targets, retention thresholds, CAC payback, and evidence thresholds from actual merchant data.
